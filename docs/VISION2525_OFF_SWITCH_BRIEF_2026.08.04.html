The Off Switch

How this system can be ended, what happens to the money when it is, and the risk that made building it necessary.

Every institution that ever outlived its usefulness had one thing in common: by the time everyone agreed it should stop, there was no longer any way to stop it.

Analysis and architecture only · not legal, tax or investment advice

This brief is for someone who has not read the ledger and does not intend to. It answers four questions: what we built, how the money unwinds, why the platform exists at all, and what we are still getting wrong.

Part one

What we built, and the flaw that prompted it

Vision 2525 is designed to last five hundred years. That ambition creates a specific danger, and for thirty-six revisions we built directly into it without noticing.

The framework carries five legal shields. No equity, so it cannot be acquired. An asset lock, so value cannot be extracted. Federated domestic personality, so no single government can shut it down. Recognition that cannot be purchased. A funded reserve behind every promise. Together they make the system very hard for anyone outside it to stop.

⚠ The flaw

We had built five defences against other people ending this system, and no way for the people inside it to end it themselves. That is not a durable institution. That is the exact shape of every organisation that stopped being useful and could not be stopped — and we had built it while congratulating ourselves on the shields.

Releases 37 and 38 close it. Anyone may petition to switch the system off. The vote is unweighted — one person, one vote. Nothing anyone has contributed counts, including money. If it carries, the reserve unwinds by a published formula, the obligations already earned are paid in full, and what remains is locked in a trust that only a restart can open.

A brake, never a throttle

Gate 2 lets the Innovation Leader slow or stop a shutdown. It can never cause one — no combination of leader and council can end this system without a carried mandate from ordinary people. So the veto is structurally incapable of destroying the framework. It can only preserve it against a decision it believes is premature. That asymmetry is the only reason a single-person veto is acceptable here, and it expires: the counter-signature belongs to the founding 7 + 3 term and lapses automatically ten years after cumulative Seed Token proceeds first reach $1,000,000. It also lapses if it is not exercised within ninety days — an off switch that can be disabled by not answering the phone is not an off switch.

The formula, in plain terms

If the system stops, the reserve does not become a pot to be divided among whoever is loudest. It splits three ways, in strict order, and the first claim is not a funder’s.

✓ The guarantee outlives the vote

Someone two years into a five-year payout does not lose the remaining three because a vote went a certain way. “Guaranteed” means nothing if a majority can cancel it, and a shutdown is precisely where that promise would be cheapest to break. So it is ranked first, ahead of every funder.

The Continuity Index

Seed Token holders are repaid in proportion to how much the platform grew during the years they were funding it. Four measures, weighted equally: Revenue · Gross Profit · Operating Income · R&D Spend.

for each measure m:   Δm(y) = m(year it stops) − m(year you bought)
                      share(y) = Δm(y) ÷ Δm(founding year)

Index(y)  = average of the four shares
Payout    = your tokens × Index, as a share of everyone else's

An early holder funded the whole climb, so their index is near 1.0. Someone who joined last year funded only the final stretch, so theirs is low. Someone who bought on the day it stopped funded none of it, and their index is zero.

Why differences, and not a growth multiple

The obvious version is a growth ratio — today’s revenue divided by revenue in the year you bought. It breaks immediately. Operating income is negative in the early years of anything worth building, and dividing by a negative number produces −144.0: a figure with no meaning that would silently invert a founder’s claim into a debt. Measuring the difference works cleanly through zero and stays orderly from first holder to last.

If it comes back

A restart is not a reversal — that would make the vote theatre, a pause anyone could wait out. Dormancy of one, two or three years is drawn at random from the final state hash, because a known restart date would be organised around: capital parked against it, coalitions assembled for it, the timing chosen for a political season rather than a readiness. Earliest restart is the drawn period plus one preparation year.

The condition that matters

You may not restart the system you switched off. Every restart petitioner must submit a concrete improvement, and the restarted version must differ from the switched-off version in at least one ratified way. That is not a promise anyone has to trust — it is a mechanical comparison between two releases, and if it comes back empty the restart is void. Ending this system is therefore not a death. It is a forced learning cycle with a real cost attached.

Part three

The risk this platform addresses

Everything above is machinery. Here is what the machinery is for.

Large groups of people are very good at generating ideas and very bad at deciding between them. The failure is not a shortage of intelligence; it is that the process of deciding is captured by whoever has the most of something — money, tenure, volume, proximity to the decision. The result looks like consensus and is not. Good ideas from people without standing never reach the surface, and by the time anyone measures the outcome, the decision is unattributable.

Vision 2525 attacks that with one structural rule and one economic one.

The one-sentence version

The platform exists so that a group of people can decide something together without the decision being quietly bought, can see who contributed what, and can end the whole arrangement if it stops serving them.

The economic half is worth stating precisely, because it is where most frameworks of this kind quietly fail. Contributed time mints a unit of recognition at a rate that is identical everywhere on earth — one full-time year of contribution reaches the annual ceiling exactly, whether that year was worked in Lagos or Seattle. The local wage still applies, but only at settlement, when the unit converts to money, and the rate is frozen at the moment the work was recorded so it cannot be re-priced by anyone who later holds it.

✓ Why that ordering matters

Until recently our own code applied the local wage at the wrong moment. It meant reaching the same ceiling cost 29,409 hours in Nigeria and 614 in Washington State — a 47.9× spread, against a stated goal of helping as many people as possible reach it. It is now 2,080 hours everywhere. We found that in our own system, published it, and fixed it, and this brief mentions it because a framework that only reports its successes is not reporting.

Part four

What we are still getting wrong

Five items are open. Three need a decision from a person; two are operational and can be closed this week. All of them are published in the ledger’s own defect register rather than discovered later by someone else.

Open itemWhy it mattersNeeds
The payout can wipe out a late funder Index falls to zero in the shutdown year. If a shutdown starts to look likely, the rational act is to stop funding — which drains the reserve and makes shutdown likelier. It is self-fulfilling at the worst possible moment. A principal floor before the index split. Proposed, not yet adopted.
The shutdown thresholds are unratified Quorum, carry and confirmation are proposals. An unratified threshold is not a threshold. Council ratification.
The reserve is short of the long-horizon target Sustaining nine years of security for a billion people needs a corpus that a founding treasury does not reach on its own. It closes only through long-run compounding. A funding decision: full pre-funding, or duration-matched.
Two caps carry no numbers Overhead and institutional gifts are described as capped with no figure stated anywhere. An undefined cap is not a cap. Publish the numbers.
Credential hygiene on the master key A short numeric PIN is brute-forceable without a lockout, and a PIN typed into a live session is not truly a second factor. Hardware-bound second factor, attempt lockout, and rotation before first use.
The disclosure rule that governs this framework says a hidden gap is worse than a known one — and that it applies to us before it applies to anyone else.
What we are asking for

Three decisions, in order of how much they block:

  1. The principal floor. Should a late funder be repaid their contribution before the growth-indexed split begins? We think yes, and it changes a stated rule, so it is not ours to change.
  2. The five shutdown thresholds. Until they are set, the off switch is a specification rather than a working mechanism.
  3. The reserve fork. Pre-fund the full guarantee, or match funding to the duration of obligations as they arise.

Why any of this is possible

A system can only afford an off switch if stopping does not destroy it. This one can, for a structural reason: the record is not stored, it is reconstructed. Every version of this framework can be rebuilt exactly, in any browser, from an append-only list of changes — including the version that was running on the day it was switched off. Nothing is overwritten, no correction erases the mistake before it, and any two versions can be compared directly.

That is what makes ending it survivable rather than final. A framework meant to run for five centuries will be wrong at some point, probably more than once. The question was never how to prevent that. It was whether being wrong could be made recoverable.
