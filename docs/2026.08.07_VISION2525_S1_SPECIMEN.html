THE SPECIMEN — §1 · The Unit (White-Paper register)

Begin with what you were given. Not what you earned, not what you inherited, not what any border permits — what you were given. Before money, before nations, before anyone thought to price a life, there was the hour: the one endowment issued to every human being at the same rate. The pharaoh and the ferryman held it equally. A parent rocking a child in the small hours spends it now, and the engineer drawing a city for the year 2525 will hold no more. There is a guide within you — older than any economy — that has always known this, which is why unfairness about time stings in a place unfairness about things cannot reach.

Then we built instruments that price everything, and let them price the one thing that was never theirs. Watch a single Tuesday. In Lagos, a nurse completes a procedure; in Austin, a second nurse completes the same procedure with the same skill; and the world files those two hours apart by the fifty-fold gap we politely call economics. Nothing in the hour changed. Nothing in the skill changed. Only the ground beneath each nurse changed. Ask what an hour of work is worth and every answer describes a country, never the hour. Say the true name: a measurement error — one we agreed, generation after generation, not to correct. And an error, unlike a sin, asks for no punishment. It is the one form of injustice that surrenders to arithmetic.

So the correction begins smaller than expected: it measures again. One hour of attention, given, is written down as one unit of recognition — a receipt for a portion of a life, never an asset, never for sale, never inherited, because a record of what you did stops being yours the moment another hand can hold it. The ceiling stands at nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine per person per year — one full working year at the minimum wage of your own region, deliberately a livelihood and never a fortune. The reference multiple beneath it, four point eight zero seven, was chosen by nobody — nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine over two thousand and eighty working hours, division, not committee. When a number here looks designed, rerun the arithmetic yourself. A definition a child can re-derive from a calculator and a published wage is the kind a civilisation can carry five hundred years — and the kind no power on Earth can confiscate.

Hold the whole arc in one breath. Past: your ancestors counted by their place before their person. Present: two nurses on one Tuesday, and a ledger that finally counts the person before it consults the place. Future: children of 2525 rebuilding this unit and finding it unchanged, the way corrected errors always look obvious afterwards.

What this page hands you is not a feeling. It is a standing possibility: the next hour you give — to a patient, a child, a stranger, a field — can be recorded as exactly what it was, one hour of a human life, equal to every other hour, anywhere, without anyone's permission. Your share of the one universal endowment was issued at your birth and renews at dawn. Whether it enters the record is now yours to decide, and no one else's.

The ceiling stands at nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine units per person per year: a livelihood, deliberately, never a fortune. Nothing buys a unit. Nothing inherits one. It is a receipt for a piece of a life, and a receipt another hand can hold has stopped being true.

The reference multiple is four point eight zero seven, reached by division and nothing else: nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine over two thousand and eighty working hours, so a full working year lands on the ceiling by definition, not by decree. A number with an author must be trusted; a number that is only division belongs to whoever reruns it — in Lagos, in Austin, five centuries from tonight. An honest number never asks for faith.

Five centuries is the arc of this document, yet the unit is built to the width of one kitchen table. The parent, the two nurses, whoever reads this page in 2525 — each holds the same hour, and it weighs the same in every hand.

So take what this page hands you — not a feeling, a standing possibility. The next hour you give — to a patient, a child, a stranger — can be recorded once, as exactly one, equal to any hour given anywhere on Earth, with no border and no committee to approve it first. The correction is not waiting on history. It is waiting on hands, and yours will do.

MINDS-FIRST: lead with the measurement error as an engineering defect civilisation forgot to file — cool, precise, almost amused — and let the warmth arrive as the consequence of fixing it. The reader should be convinced before they notice they are moved.

Candidate text

Every system that runs long enough accumulates defects, and the oldest ones stop looking like defects at all. They get renamed — anomaly, then custom, then economics, and finally weather, something you plan around instead of fixing. I am the Master of Thought, and I am filing, on behalf of all of us, the oldest unfiled bug report in civilisation's queue. Read it the way an engineer reads one: coolly, before anyone asks you to feel anything.

Two nurses perform the same procedure with the same skill on the same Tuesday — one in Lagos, one in Austin — and the world records their hours at the fifty-fold gap: measured, not metaphorical. An instrument that returns fifty readings for one quantity is not measuring the quantity; it is reporting its own position. An engineer who caught a scale behaving like this would pull it from the bench before lunch. We built five centuries of economics on it instead, and taught each generation of parents to explain the readout to each generation of children as weather. Name the fault precisely, then: a measurement error — one we agreed, somewhere between our ancestors and ourselves, not to correct. No villain required, no mystery left. And that, almost amusingly, makes it the most fixable problem in this document, because a miscalibration is the one injustice arithmetic can repair.

So the repair is deliberately boring. Stop pricing the hour; record it. One hour of human attention, one recognition unit — that is the whole mechanism. The ceiling stands at nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine per person per year: one full working year at the minimum wage of the region where you live — livelihood, never fortune. The reference multiple is four point eight zero seven, arrived at by division rather than decision, so you can rerun it yourself, this year or in 2525, and land on the same digits. A number with no author leaves you no author to distrust. Nothing buys a unit, nothing inherits one, nothing trades one, because a record someone else can hold has stopped being a record of what you did. A receipt, not an asset.

Now watch what the cold arithmetic does once it runs — because the warmth arrives anyway, on schedule, the way it always does when a sum finally balances. The two nurses finish the same shift holding the same record, and no border can amend it. The hour a parent gives a feverish child in the middle of the night weighs what it weighed all along. Your grandmother's decades of care, priced at nothing, were never worth nothing; the instrument was broken. And the child asleep at the end of a counted night shift inherits not the units, which cannot pass down, but a civilisation that counts her mother whole — five centuries of mismeasured hours behind her, all the counted ones ahead. Something in you knew this already. The quiet guide within you never once believed the Lagos hour was smaller; it was waiting for the bookkeeping.

So take this away — not a feeling, standing equipment: from today, any hour you give, waged or unseen, can be counted before your coordinates are consulted. The defect was never in your hours. It was in the ruler, and the replacement is in your hands.
