On the renaming — and a caution the review must answer to itself

The previous pass warned that “every additional layer is a place for two documents to disagree.” That warning still stands, and this revision adds two layers. It is worth being explicit about why that is not a contradiction.

Documents 0, 0.5, 1, the Charter, Policies and Regional are all normative — a human writes a rule, and two humans can write conflicting rules. Every normative layer added multiplies the surface for disagreement. Document 0.75 is different in kind: it is derived. If it is generated from the layers above, it cannot contradict them, because it has no independent content — it is a projection, plus a test that fails when the projection stops matching reality.

So the count is not eight normative layers and one map. It is seven normative layers and one generated artifact that guards them. That is the discipline that makes the addition safe, and it is the single condition on accepting 0.75. Hand-author it and it becomes an eighth normative layer — a fourth place for a term to be defined, and therefore a fourth place for it to drift.

Why the conformance protocol moved into 0.75

The previous review proposed a standalone Conformance & Inheritance Protocol. eXeL AI's Dependency & Inheritance Map covers the same territory from the other side: the map states what inherits from what; the protocol states what must pass before that inheritance changes. They are the noun and the verb of one idea.

Kept apart, they drift from each other — the exact failure this whole review is about. Merged, the map gains teeth and the protocol gains a subject. This is the model docs/2525-core/WIREFRAME-CORE.md already runs for geometry: twelve named primitives (U-WF-01…12) plus a parallel development protocol stating that “a change to a U-WF primitive must pass BOTH domains' verification sheets,” plus objective pass criteria — determinism hash stable across five runs, render within the HAL budget, SSSES per pillar. Naming and enforcement live in one document there, and that document works.

§ 3

Purpose of each document layer D#2

Decision required before drafting

Document 0 should be a registry, not an essay. One entry per term: id · term · definition · boundary (what it is NOT) · class · supersedes · owner · version. Prose renders from the registry; the registry is never derived from prose.

This is the highest-leverage choice in the hierarchy, because it decides whether a downstream system can check that it used a term correctly or merely claim it did — and because §4 shows the framework has already failed that check three times without noticing.

The mechanism exists and is proven at scale: frontend/lib/lexicon-data.ts holds the app's key registry across the 33 supported languages (frontend/lib/constants.ts:53) with a documented fallback chain (translation → English default → raw key, frontend/lib/lexicon-context.tsx:182) and an admin approval gate. Document 0 should inherit that machinery rather than invent a second one.

The repository already runs a disciplined citation scheme — CRS-##.## with .IN/.OUT identifiers, and domain-scoped variants U-WF-##, SEC-##.##, ARC-##.##. § 5

Change classes — Immutable · Stable · Adaptive eXeL

eXeL AI's three-class scheme is adopted, with one structural correction. It is the cheapest governance tool in this entire review: it tells anyone proposing a change which document to open before they have to understand the hierarchy.

The correction — and the rule it generalises to

The proposed Immutable list includes Qualification and Replay. But execution_modes.py:21-22 sets DEFAULT_MAX_RISK = 0.30 and DEFAULT_MIN_CONFIDENCE = 0.70 — the thresholds a result must clear to be auto-accepted. Those numbers are tunable and should be, and they are unambiguously part of qualification.

So qualification is not one thing in one class. It is two:

The obligation is immutable; the number is adaptive. The right to qualify and the duty to qualify before automating never change. The threshold at which qualification passes changes whenever evidence says it should.

This rule generalises, and it resolves the same tension in three other places: Compensation (entitlement vs settlement, §10), Stewardship (the 7+3 split vs the seat count), and Membership (status vs joining procedure). Wherever a concept feels like it belongs in two classes at once, it is two concepts and needs two entries in Document 0.

ClassLives inChanged byContents
ImmutableVision · Doc 0 · Doc 0.5 · CharterNear-unamendable; supermajority + Charter reviewPurpose · Human Authority · the duty to Replay · the duty to Qualify · Trust · Evidence · Reversal · Disclosure of Absence · Stewardship principles · Anti-Capture · Purpose Lock
StableConstitution · Doc 0.75Constitutional amendment processArticles · governance structure · membership status · amendment rules · the decision sequence · inheritance edges · extension points
AdaptivePolicies · Regional · Project FrameworksOrdinary operational decision, loggedQualification thresholds · risk/confidence guardrails · economic formulas · engineering standards · software architecture · regional legal wrappers · domain models

The test for which class a change belongs to

Ask what breaks if it changes. Immutable: the framework stops being itself — someone who trusted it yesterday would not recognise it today. Stable: the framework is recognisably the same, but who decides or how they decide has moved. Adaptive: a competent team could tune it next quarter and no one outside the team would need to be told.

A practical proxy that agrees with the test in nearly every case: if it contains a number, it is Adaptive. The exceptions are so few they should be enumerated in Document 0 by hand — the 7+3 stewardship split and the amendment thresholds are the obvious ones.

§ 7

Universal vs jurisdiction-specific D#5

The single most effective de-risking move available: Regional documents implement numbered obligations; they never create them. Structurally this means Regional is a conformance matrix, not a set of parallel rewrites.

ObligationUniversal — ConstitutionLocal — Regional matrix
CompensationEntitlement exists, is earned by contribution, and is denominated in timeCurrency, tax treatment, employment classification, payment rail

Only the right-hand column changes per jurisdiction, and each cell cites the V25-##.## clause it satisfies. A new country becomes one new column, reviewed by local counsel, with nothing above it touched. If adding a jurisdiction ever requires editing the Constitution, the boundary has been drawn in the wrong place.

This review makes no claim of legal sufficiency in any jurisdiction, and the structure above is deliberately designed so it does not have to: the universal layers state obligations in plain governance terms, and qualified local counsel supplies the legal instrument that satisfies each one. The matrix is the interface between the two, and it is the only artifact counsel needs to read.

The distinction that keeps this maintainable

The duty to retain is constitutional. The retention mechanism is policy. Put the mechanism in the Constitution and every storage migration for the next five centuries becomes a constitutional amendment. Put the duty in policy and the first cost-cutting quarter deletes the record.

Every one of the ten items above splits cleanly along that line, which is a good sign that the line is real and not an artifact of this review.

One unresolved question that must be settled before 0.5 is written

§ 9

Human Authority architecture D#7

Document 0.5 is warranted — and it is not speculative

The strongest argument for 0.5 is that a working version of it is already running. backend/app/core/rcore/execution_modes.py states the invariant in its own header: “The SAME operation runs in every mode — only the APPROVER / automation changes.” Then it implements exactly that: manual/assisted → a human must approve; semi_automated → a human selects among variants; automated → auto-accepted only inside a guardrail (risk ≤ 0.30 and confidence ≥ 0.70), otherwise held for a human.

That is “Qualify before automating” already enforced in code, with the human never removed — only relocated, from deciding each case to setting the guardrail. 0.5 does not invent a decision architecture. It writes down the one the system already has, so future systems inherit it instead of reinventing it differently.

Three gaps in the proposed sequence

  1. Evidence
  2. AI Analysis
  3. Human Review
  4. Human Decision
  5. Replay
  6. Qualification
  7. Disclosure — state what was not covered, not verified, or dropped. U-WF-09 already forbids silent caps; this is that duty placed in the sequence rather than left to engineering culture.
  8. Learning
  9. Knowledge Accumulation
  10. Reversal — the arc back. Knowledge Accumulation must be able to reach the original decision and undo it, not merely inform the next one.

Gap 1 — it is a ladder, and it needs to be a cycle

The sequence ends at Knowledge Accumulation with nowhere to go. But the question that matters most is what happens when accumulated knowledge shows the decision was wrong. As written, learning flows forward into the next decision and the bad decision stands. Reversal closes the loop, and without it the architecture can learn but cannot correct — which is the difference between a system that improves and one that merely accumulates.

Gap 2 — no step obliges disclosure

Every step in the proposed sequence describes something produced. None describes something admitted. In a chain that ends in Knowledge Accumulation, an omission at any step becomes part of the permanent record as though it were complete. This is the failure mode the project keeps meeting, and it already has a rule — it just lives in a geometry spec rather than in governance.

Gap 3 — a single linear sequence cannot express modes

This is the one that matters architecturally. The running system does not have one decision path; it has seven modes, and the sequence compresses differently in each. A linear 0.5 would be contradicted by execution_modes.py on the day it was written.

The fix is small and makes 0.5 considerably stronger: define the sequence and the mode ladder, bound by one law —

Proposed invariant for Document 0.5

A mode may compress the sequence. A mode may never remove Human Authority — only relocate it, from per-decision to per-guardrail. Where authority has been relocated, the guardrail must be human-set, published, and reversible.

This is already true in the code. Stating it in 0.5 makes it true for every Vision 2525 system that inherits it, including ones written by people who never read the polling backend.

Where Human Authority is defined — and where it is not

Define it once, in 0.5. The Constitution's Article 6 cites it. The Charter protects it. Policies implement it. Project Frameworks may narrow it but never widen it. This supersedes the previous review's recommendation to move Human Authority to Article 3 — that was a fix for it being buried among eleven other articles, and 0.5 fixes the same problem more directly by putting it above them all.

§ 10

Economic architecture

The revised chain — Participation → Contribution → Qualification → Responsibility → Capability → Trust → Compensation — is a real improvement on monetary escalation, and the addition of Trust before Compensation is the right instinct: it makes standing, not spend, the thing that accrues.

Softening a previous recommendation

The previous review proposed inserting Recognition between Contribution and Qualification. Trust now absorbs most of what that was reaching for, so this is downgraded from a recommendation to a consideration.

What remains unaddressed is narrow but real: the chain has no step where a contribution is acknowledged before it is judged. Everything between Contribution and Trust is evaluative. Unrecognised contribution is the most common reason volunteer-basis systems lose their best contributors, and it costs nothing to name.

The split that keeps regional law out of the Constitution

Compensation must be two terms, not one — the same rule §5 derives from qualification:

This is not theoretical. Before it was corrected in 526fb6e, a shipped SDK demo distributed a donation at “local rate” and paid a Nigerian contributor $0.34/hour against a US contributor's $7.25/hour — a 21× gap for an identical hour of work. The 웃 unit is denominated in time precisely so that an hour is an hour everywhere; only settlement is local. Collapsing entitlement and settlement into one term is what made that outcome expressible in the first place.

The condition, restated because it is the whole value of this layer

Generate this map; do not maintain it. A hand-maintained dependency map is accurate on the day it is written and misleading within a quarter — and a misleading map is worse than none, because people stop checking the source.

§ 13

Long-term governance assessment D#9

The principal governance risk in the revised hierarchy

Documents 0 and 0.5 sit above the Constitution, so the Constitution's Amendments article cannot reach them. As drawn, the framework's most powerful layers are its least amendable — not by design, but as a side effect of ordering.

That is a capture vector, and a subtle one. Whoever controls the definitions controls every clause that cites them, and no constitutional process can dislodge them. It is the inverse of the usual failure: not a Constitution too easy to amend, but a foundation impossible to.

Fix: the Amendments article must explicitly govern Documents 0, 0.5, the Charter and itself, each with its own named threshold — highest for 0.5 and the Charter, high for 0 and the Constitution, ordinary for everything below. Un-amendable is not the same as durable. Durable means hard to change and possible to change.

Who owns Document 0

Unspecified in the memorandum, and it should not stay that way. A registry with no owner is how the three defects in §4 arose. § 14

Long-term maintainability assessment D#10

The question is whether someone in 2075 — with no access to anyone who wrote this — can read the stack, understand it, change it safely, and prove the change was safe. Assessed against that, the revised hierarchy does well on three of four axes.

ProvabilityStrong — the framework's real advantageDeterminism hashes, SSSES per pillar, audit trails, 2,212 executed tests. Governance can inherit a verification culture most frameworks never have.
Drift resistanceWeak todayThe only axis that fails. Three live conflicts (§4), all of which accumulated silently. This is precisely what 0.75's generated drift check fixes, and it is the strongest reason to accept that layer.

The maintainability metric worth adopting

One number, checkable in CI, that would have caught all three defects in §4 before they shipped:

Proposed

Count of terms defined in more than one authoritative location. Target: zero. Tested, not audited.

It is currently at least three. A governance framework that measures its own consistency will outlive one that merely asserts it — and this project already has the CI discipline to run the check, which is the part most organisations lack.

A note on scale

Nine layers is at the upper bound of what stays legible. This review recommends adding no further layers after 0.75 — and, in particular, that future needs be met by extension points declared in 0.75 rather than new documents. That is the mechanism that lets the framework grow without the hierarchy growing, and it is the same discipline WIREFRAME-CORE uses to serve Architect and Security from twelve primitives rather than two parallel specs.

§ 16

Master of Thought — synthesis

Architectural strengths

The ordering is right and is now proven right rather than argued right — three live defects in the framework's own foundation (§4) are exactly what a definitions-first sequence prevents. Document 0.5 is the strongest single addition: it is not a new idea but the articulation of one the system already runs, which means it will be inherited correctly instead of reinvented. And the framework brings something most governance projects never have — a verification culture, with determinism hashes, audit trails and 2,212 executed tests. Governance that can be tested is governance that can be trusted at a distance of centuries.

Architectural risks

Two, in order. First, definitional drift — demonstrated three times, accumulating silently, and the only one of the four maintainability axes that currently fails. Second, the un-amendable foundation (§13): by placing 0 and 0.5 above the Constitution the hierarchy put its most powerful layers beyond its own amendment process. Both have small, specific fixes available now and no good fix later.

Missing foundational concepts

Four: Mode (the code has seven; the vocabulary has none), Reversal, Disclosure of Absence, and Provenance. Each is already load-bearing in the running system. None is a philosophical addition — they are things the framework already does that its vocabulary cannot yet name, which is the most reliable signature of a term that belongs in Document 0.

Sequencing improvements

Draft 0 and 0.5 completely; draft the Constitution to citation completeness; generate 0.75 rather than writing it; defer the Charter and Regional until one project has demonstrably inherited the stack. Prove inheritance on Polling — where the cubes, the audit trail and the CI already exist — before writing for five systems. An inheritance contract that has been exercised once is worth more than one that has been reviewed five times.

Governance improvements

Extend Article 11 to govern Documents 0, 0.5, the Charter and itself, with a named threshold for each. Give Document 0 a named steward role. Require compensation clauses to cite the purpose clause they serve. Add Evidence, Reversal and Disclosure as articles. Split entitlement from settlement. Every one of these is a paragraph now; several are unfixable from inside the system later.

R-CORE implications

Governance should inherit the pattern WIREFRAME-CORE already proves rather than invent a parallel one: shared contract, domain-scoped extension, mandatory re-verification on change, objective pass criteria. The Project Frameworks layer is what makes this work for five systems, and mechanical domain prefixes are what make “extend but never redefine” enforceable rather than merely stated.

Maintainability over decades

Strong on legibility, changeability and provability; weak on drift resistance — which is the one that compounds. A framework meant to last five centuries needs to detect its own inconsistency automatically, because no human process survives that long. That is the argument for 0.75 being generated, and for the drift metric being a test rather than an audit.

Readiness

The structural question is closed. What remains is vocabulary, and vocabulary is both faster to fix and far more expensive to skip — every document written before the terms are settled inherits their ambiguity permanently.
