Verdict

The structure you want is achievable. The token you asked for is not — and you do not need it.

A $1 token that multiplies to $10 cannot be non-security in the SEC, MAS, CVM, SEBI, CBR or VARA regimes. All apply substance over form. Worse, if the $10 comes from later buyers rather than outside capital, it is a pyramid — criminal, not merely unregistered. That path is closed.

But your actual mandate — funded by the people, un-hoardable, politically unassailable — is reachable, and you are closer than you think. The Accords already specify the funding model (ATLANTIS_ACCORDS.md:69-77), and SI_tokens = floor(active_minutes) is already the strongest non-security fact pattern available: a token that cannot be bought cannot be hoarded, cannot be a security, and cannot be captured.

Six defects stand between you and that structure, and one is severe: 웃 is purchasable with money today, and 웃 carries governance weight. An investment organisation could buy governance influence right now — in direct contradiction of the Accords' strongest clause. Everything else in this document is secondary to closing that door.

The organising insight. Defensibility does not come from what this framework claims. It comes from what it permanently gives up. Each renunciation removes a regulator and forecloses an attack line at the same time — so the critic's argument becomes unavailable rather than merely answerable. §2 is that table, and it is the spine of everything after it.

§ 1

The constraint, stated once

"Raises $10 per $1" and "not a security" cannot both be true. The test is substantially the same everywhere: money paid into a common enterprise with an expectation of profit from the efforts of others. Requirement two is the trigger. No wrapper, foundation, or offshore domicile removes it — regulators look through form to substance, and Telegram, Kik and LBRY each lost precisely the "it's a utility token" argument.

This framework does not design: a token sale marketed on appreciation; any structure whose returns depend on later entrants; sanctions or anti-money-laundering evasion; unlicensed money transmission; or means to operate where an activity is lawfully banned.

And here is why that costs you nothing

Under "we the people, never corporations or investment funds," the securities problem largely disappears on its own. A voluntary contribution carrying no economic right is not a security in any of the seven jurisdictions. The instrument you were trying to engineer around was never needed for the mission you actually described.

Why this is stronger than a legal opinion

A legal opinion says "we believe this is lawful." A renunciation says "the thing you are worried about is structurally impossible here." The first invites argument; the second ends it. It is also the cheaper posture over a five-century horizon, because renunciations do not need to be re-litigated every time a statute changes.

The cost is real and should be stated plainly: these are one-way doors. A framework that renounces equity can never raise venture capital; one that renounces distributable surplus can never pay a dividend. That is the trade, and it is the correct one for this mission.

§ 3

The legal form

No legal-entity work exists anywhere in the corpus — no steward-ownership, asset lock, purpose trust or foundation model appears in any document. This is genuinely greenfield, and it is the load-bearing gap: every renunciation in §2 is a promise until an entity form makes it structural.

The family of forms that matches what you have already designed is steward-ownership — the legal expression of exactly your 7-year leader, councils, contribution-based standing, and livelihood-not-wealth compensation. Four properties do the work:

  • No equity → nothing to acquire
    An entity with no shares cannot be bought, raided or taken over. This is the structural answer to “investment organisations hoarding.” Not a policy against acquisition — an absence of the thing an acquirer would buy.
  • Asset lock → nothing to extract
    Assets may only ever transfer to another purpose-locked body, never to individuals. Statutory in some jurisdictions (the UK Community Interest Company asset lock is the clearest example); contractual in others.
  • Control separated from capital → nothing to capture
    Economic rights and governance rights are different instruments by construction. This is the Contribution ≠ Funding principle given legal force rather than stated as an aspiration.
  • Federated legal personality → nothing to switch off
    A domestic organisation in each signatory region, not one foreign entity soliciting inward. Answers the foreign-influence attack and removes the single point any one government can close. See §16.

Long-lived precedents exist across industrial ownership (non-distributing foundations holding operating companies), community enterprise (statutory asset-locked forms) and modern steward-ownership. They are presented here as patterns with trade-offs, not a recommendation of one vehicle — the choice is jurisdiction-specific and belongs to counsel.

The question counsel must be asked first

Not “which entity should we use?” but “in this jurisdiction, which form makes the §2 renunciations irreversible by a future board?” A renunciation a successor can undo is a preference, not a protection — and the whole framework rests on their permanence.

The six defects

Defect 1 · severe · 웃 is purchasable with money today

cube8_tokens/service.py:75 and :488 state: “This is the ONLY way to earn 웃 tokens — real money → real value”, implementing 웃 = $ ÷ 7.25.

Because Token_Governance_Math.md:76 gives HI governance weight (W_base = SI + HI × 0.5), there is a live path from money → 웃 → governance influence. That directly contradicts ATLANTIS_ACCORDS.md:73 — “No contribution buys governance influence, from any source, ever” — and your own “never corporation or investment funds.” An investment organisation could buy governance weight today. It also supplies the first element of the securities test, destroying the framework's best legal asset.

Precision: the purchase path is live in code; HI_weight_factor is spec-only, not yet implemented. So this is a latent breach that activates the moment the governance spec ships — which is why it must be fixed before, not after.

Fix: 웃 is earned by contributed time only, never purchased. Money in is a donation that mints no token and carries no weight — which is what the Accords already say. If a payer needs acknowledgement, issue a non-transferable, non-governance receipt: a fourth, inert class.

Defect 2 · a 21.3× cross-border arbitrage

Because HI settles at the holder's local rate, 9999 HI earned in Nigeria settles at $3,399.66 but redeems in Texas for $72,492.75 — a 21.3× gain from moving the token rather than from contributing anything. Transfers are explicitly contemplated: Token_Governance_Math.md:122-129 exists to govern them. A well-capitalised actor could farm low-rate jurisdictions and redeem high — hoarding by geography.

Fix: stamp the settlement rate on the append-only ledger entry at mint time, from the earner's jurisdiction. Hours are universal; the rate that converts them freezes the moment they are earned. One field. Preserves transferability, kills the arbitrage.

Defect 3 · the local rate is applied at the wrong stage

Your model is correct and confirmed by its own arithmetic: 1 HI ≡ 1 hour at your local minimum wage, so 9999 × 7.25 = $72,492.75 exactly. The token count is identical worldwide; only settlement differs — and every region lands on the same multiple:

RegionMin wage/hr9999 HI settles at× local full-time year (2,080 h)
Texas7.25$72,492.754.8×

The code does not do this. cube5_gateway/service.py:92-94 computes minutes × (local_wage/60), so 40 hours mints Texas 290 HI · Brazil 63.2 HI · Nigeria 13.6 HI where your model says 40 everywhere — the same 21.3× spread, now for identical work. This is the Lagos/Austin defect of 526fb6e resurfacing in the ledger rather than in demo copy. Separately, cube8_tokens/service.py:76 hard-codes HI_RATE_PER_HOUR = 7.25 for every jurisdiction with no rate lookup at all, and Token_Governance_Math.md:20-28 encodes the same error.

Fix: the local rate belongs at settlement, not at mint. Apply it once, in the right place, and your model works exactly as described. Keep 9999.

Defect 4 · the anti-hoarding caps have no numbers

ATLANTIS_ACCORDS.md:75: “Administrative overhead is capped. Corporate and institutional gifts are welcomed and capped.” No figure appears anywhere in the corpus. An undefined cap is not a cap — and it is the first clause a hostile critic will test, because it is the one the Accords rely on to keep large donors from acquiring standing.

Fix: publish two numbers — a maximum share of total funding from any single institutional source, and a maximum administrative overhead ratio. Both belong in the Adaptive class (tunable, logged), but they must exist.

Defect 5 · the influence cap is defeated by the reputation multiplier

W_damped caps a user at influence_cap = 0.15. But effective_governance_weight = W_damped × reputation_score, where reputation_score = consistency (≤2.0) × tenure (≤3.0) = ≤6.0. Multiplying after the cap lifts a single actor to roughly 90% of the pre-cap pool — and the person who maximises tenure is definitionally your 7-year leader, precisely who the cap exists to constrain.

Precision: _apply_influence_cap is implemented (cube7_ranking/ranking_aggregation.py:59); reputation_score, tenure_factor, damping_factor and HI_weight_factor are spec-only. A specification defect to fix before implementation, not a live bug.

Fix: cap last, or cap the product. The cap must be the final operation.

Defect 6 · whoever defines the metrics controls the money

Compensation is “proportional based on actual project metrics.” The Accords protect governance from money; nothing protects the metric definitions. That is a new capture vector this design creates — subtler than buying votes and considerably harder to detect, because it looks like accounting.

Fix: metric-definition authority carries the same change threshold as governance itself, and every definition change is logged with its effect on the compensation pool computed before it takes effect.

§ 13

The compensation model

Confirmed correct — and it is the anti-capture engine

Livelihood, not lump-sum wealth. This is the single most important choice in the whole design, because wealth concentration is the mechanism of capture. A ceiling of 9999 HI — 4.8× local minimum wage, identical multiple in every country — means no participant can accumulate enough to buy influence, and no outsider can offer enough to make betrayal rational.

It is also the answer to the “insiders are enriching themselves” attack in §5, and it works precisely because it is published and capped rather than negotiated and private.

The two fixes are Defects 2 and 3: settle at the earner's stamped rate, and apply the local rate at settlement rather than at mint. Keep 9999 exactly as specified — it encodes a full-time year at 4.8× local minimum wage, and the number is the operator's own.

One design question worth deciding explicitly

Is the 9999 ceiling per person or per role? If per person, a contributor working across three projects hits the same ceiling as one working on a single project — which is correct for anti-hoarding but may under-reward breadth. If per role, the ceiling is defeated by holding several roles. Per person is recommended, with breadth recognised through governance standing rather than through additional compensation.

§ 14

A one-third R&D commitment is unusually high and genuinely signals innovation priority — typical technology firms run 10–20%. But 33.333% of what? The supplied design names four quantities — SoI Revenue, Gross Margin, R&D Budget, Operating Income — without saying which is the base. An undefined denominator is another instance of the drift defect class, and this one moves real money.

DenominatorMeansBehaviour

§ 15

Surviving any single government

“Cannot be stopped” has a legitimate and much stronger reading than evasion: no single point of failure that any one government can switch off. The Accords already reach for this in rejecting single-point dependencies. Four properties deliver it:

  • Federated legal personality
    A domestic organisation per region. Losing one loses one — not the whole.
  • No single rail, custodian or domicile
    Payment, custody and incorporation diversified by design rather than by accident.
  • Open-licensed outputs
    Curricula and outcomes already published openly (ATLANTIS_ACCORDS.md:87), so the work survives the organisation.
  • Radical traceability as political defence
    Every contribution followed origin → stipend → project, published monthly. Hard to attack, easy to defend.
The boundary, stated plainly

This means surviving the loss of any single jurisdiction. It does not mean operating unlawfully inside one. A chapter that cannot lawfully operate in a country closes in that country — and the framework continues everywhere else. That distinction is what makes the design defensible rather than merely durable.
