Verdict

42% of the world has no single hourly wage to denominate in — and never will.

74.6% of the table is NULL, but the NULLs are not all the same kind. 37 rows are merely unfilled — a national rate exists and research would recover it. The other 48 — 42.1% of the table — are structurally unfillable. Those jurisdictions have collective agreements, sectoral floors, occupational bands, regional rates and employer-size tiers, or publish nothing comparable at all. No research programme fills those cells, because the number being sought does not exist.

Which means wage cannot be the unit. A unit that is undefinable for 42% of jurisdictions cannot standardize anything. And the instruction already contains the answer: maximize 웃 to standardize work time. 1 웃 = the value of one hour at minimum wage — a notional unit, available in 100% of jurisdictions, needing no table, no currency, no exchange rate and no lookup. A member paid 5× the floor earns 5 웃 an hour, so the unit standardizes work time without forcing everyone onto one rate. The wage table is demoted from the unit to a settlement convenience — and everything that was hard becomes easy.

The structural conclusion

A wage-denominated 웃 works today in 25.4% of the world, and could reach 57.9% with a research effort. It stops there. The remaining 42.1% is not a backlog — those societies genuinely set pay another way, and the number does not exist to be found. A 웃 denominated in minimum-wage-hours works in 100%, today, with no research at all — the unit is notional, not a lookup.

Put the other way: a wage-denominated design would permanently exclude two of every five jurisdictions — including some of the most equitable labour markets on earth. That is the decision, and it is not close.

§ 2

웃 is the minimum-wage hour — the instruction, made structural

1 웃 ≡ the value of one hour at minimum wage. Not one hour worked — one minimum-wage-hour of value. The distinction is the whole design, because it is what lets the framework pay some members far above the floor without breaking the unit:

The formula

웃 earned = hours worked × pay multiple

A contributor paid at the floor earns 1 웃/hour. One paid at five times the floor earns 5 웃/hour. The multiple is set by role, skill and qualification — it is not a property of their country. Two people doing identical work at the same multiple earn identical 웃 in Lagos and in Austin; only what those 웃 settle to differs.

This is what “maximize 웃 to standardize work time” means carried all the way through, and it has four properties nothing currency-based can match:

PropertyConsequence
Universally definedThe unit is notional — a minimum-wage hour as an abstract measure, not any particular country's published figure. So minting works in all 103 jurisdictions, including the 13 that publish no wage at all and the four with unstable currency. Coverage is 100% by construction, not by data collection.
Needs no tableMinting needs the multiple, which the framework sets — not the wage, which the world sets. No rate lookup, no FX, no date, no fallback. The most reliable subsystem is the one that does not exist.
Cannot inflateA minimum-wage hour in 2026 buys roughly what one buys in 2525 — that is what a wage floor is. Currency-denominated ledgers do not survive five centuries; a labour-denominated one does.
Cannot be bought웃 is issued against work performed, never against money paid. This is the same property that makes it a non-security and un-hoardable — the unit and the anti-capture mechanism are the same choice.

The last row is worth sitting with. The reason 웃 resists capture and the reason it works in 103 jurisdictions are not two design decisions that happen to agree. They are one decision seen from two sides. Denominating in labour rather than currency is simultaneously the equity argument, the coverage argument, the durability argument and the securities argument.

§ 3

Mint and settle are different problems

Once 웃 is the minimum-wage hour, the wage table stops being load-bearing. It is needed only when someone converts 웃 into local money — which is optional, local, and late.

The law this establishes

A missing wage rate must never block a contribution from being recorded. Someone in Vatican City, North Korea or Uganda contributes an hour and earns 1 웃, exactly like everyone else. What they cannot immediately do is convert it — and that is a settlement question, not a recognition question.

The current code inverts this: backend/app/cubes/cube5_gateway/service.py:92-94 multiplies by the rate at mint, so a jurisdiction with no rate cannot mint correctly at all. Moving the rate to settlement fixes the equity defect and the coverage defect with the same change.

§ 4

The settlement ladder

The table's own three states are the tiers. Each is a different kind of answer, and the difference must be visible to the person being paid.

29 · 25.4%
Tier 1 — published single national hourly rate

Direct settlement. US · GB · CA · AU · NZ · NG · CM · ES · MX · CO · CL · DE · LU · BR · RU · CN · JP · KR · EG · MA · PK · TH · VN · ID · MY · PH · TR · PL · IL. Note several of these are already sub-national (Beijing, Tokyo, Bangkok, Jakarta, Metro Manila, Punjab, Region I) — the rate is published, but the jurisdiction it covers is narrower than the country.

37 · 32.5%
Tier 1 pending — a national rate exists, not yet recorded

IE · FR · PT · CZ · GR · RO · UA · PE · BO · AR and 27 others. These belong in Tier 1 once the figure is sourced and dated. They are the only rows where research is the answer — and the only ones that should be treated as a backlog.

35 · 30.7%
Tier 2 — published, but no single national rate

A rate exists; the system must choose which. Regional (CH cantons, CA Québec), sectoral (MZ · AO · BD · SV), occupational (CR · SG · SA), employer-size (HN · DO), or citizenship-linked (OM · BH · KW · SA). The selection rule is a governance decision, not a data decision — highest, median, or the one matching the contributor's declared sector — and whichever is chosen must be written down once and applied identically everywhere.

13 · 11.4%
Tier 3 — no official rate published

Collective-bargaining economies (AT · IT · SE · DK · FI · LI) where a legal minimum genuinely does not exist, and jurisdictions with no comparable figure (KP · YE · UG · RW · AE · VA). No wage anchor can be derived honestly. Settlement here requires either a declared proxy — with its provenance stated — or an honest “settlement unavailable in this jurisdiction.”

Mandatory — disclosure of tier

Every settlement states which tier produced it. A Tier 1 figure and a Tier 3 estimate must never render identically, because they are not the same kind of claim. This is U-WF-09 — “silent caps forbidden; must report dropped detail” — promoted from geometry to money.

Without it, the 42.1% quietly becomes indistinguishable from the 25.4%, and the framework's most valuable property — that it never overstates what it knows — is lost at exactly the point where money changes hands.

§ 5

Three defects this table exposes in the running system

Defect A · severe · the two tables are in different units

backend/app/core/hi_rates.py stores rates in USD. This table is in local currency. Neither file says so. Loading one into the other multiplies every non-USD rate by its exchange rate:

The implied ratios are real exchange rates — 34 THB/USD and 1,185 NGN/USD are approximately correct — which confirms the diagnosis rather than merely suggesting it. The two sources are internally consistent; they simply measure different things.

Fix: every rate carries its currency_code explicitly. There is no such thing as a bare number here. The table already has the column — the code does not.

Defect B · 91% of jurisdictions silently settle at Texas today

hi_rates.py resolves 9 non-US countries. The table lists 103 jurisdictions. DEFAULT_HUMAN_RATE = 7.25 with DEFAULT_COUNTRY = "United States" means the other 94 — 91% — silently receive the Texas rate. No warning, no flag, no disclosure.

And the nine that are covered do not align either: six appear with published numbers (in the other unit, per Defect A), Nepal and Honduras are NULL in the new table, and Cambodia is absent entirely — yet all three carry confident three-decimal values in code today.

Fix: an unresolvable jurisdiction must return unresolved, never a default. A silent fallback to one country's wage is the same defect class as a silent cap.

Defect C · the table is keyed by language, and a wage is not a property of language

Eight jurisdictions appear more than once because the key is (language, country): India ×4 (English · Hindi · Bengali · Punjabi), Switzerland ×3 (French · German · Italian), and Belgium · Canada · DRC · Cyprus · Finland · Singapore ×2 each.

Under this key, two people doing identical work in Switzerland could settle differently because one reads the interface in German and the other in French. That is arbitrary, and in employment terms it is worse than arbitrary.

Fix — two tables, joined only for display: the rate is keyed by jurisdiction (country_code + optional subdivision). The language belongs to the lexicon, which already exists and already carries 33 languages (frontend/lib/constants.ts:53). 103 rate rows, 33 language rows — not 114 conflated ones.

§ 6

Currency date is not optional

Four rows carry their own warning: Sudan and Syria are marked “currency-date control required”, Venezuela “currency value highly unstable”, Argentina “frequently adjusted”. But the problem is general, not special — every one of the 27 currencies needs a date, because a rate without one is unreproducible, and an unreproducible settlement cannot be replayed.

One mechanism, three problems

Stamping rate + currency + as_of_date + tier on the ledger entry at the moment of earning solves all of these at once:

· the cross-border arbitrage (settle at the earner's stamped rate, not the redeemer's)
· the unstable-currency problem (the rate is fixed at the moment work was done, not at redemption)
· replay (the settlement is reproducible for centuries)
· tier disclosure (§4 travels with the entry rather than being recomputed later)

Four fields on an append-only record, and the ledger becomes self-describing.

§ 8

The 9999 ceiling — and why it is progressive by construction

9999 웃 is not 9999 hours. It is 9999 minimum-wage-hours of value, which is a very different thing — and the difference produces a property worth naming, because it appears to be accidental and is in fact the most defensible feature of the whole compensation model.

QuantityDenominated inVaries by jurisdiction?
1 웃One minimum-wage hour of valueNo. Notional; identical in all 103.
웃 earned per hour workedThe pay multipleNo. Set by role and skill, not by country.
9999 웃/year ceilingMinimum-wage-hour equivalentsNo. Same ceiling for everyone.
What 9999 웃 settles toLocal currencyYes. That is settlement's job.

Where the ceiling actually bites

Pay multipleHours to reach 9999 웃Effect of the cap
1×9,999 hUnreachable — more than a year contains
2×5,000 hUnreachable in a year
3×3,333 hUnreachable in a year
4.81×2,080 hThe threshold — exactly a full-time year
5×2,000 hBinds at 96% of a work year
10×1,000 hBinds at half a work year
20×500 hBinds at a quarter of a work year
The property this creates

The ceiling is invisible to everyone paid below ~4.8× minimum wage, and progressively tighter above it. A contributor at the floor cannot hit 9999 no matter how hard they work — a year does not contain enough hours. A specialist at 20× hits it in three months.

So the cap does exactly what an anti-hoarding instrument should: it constrains the top and never touches the bottom. It permits paying rare skills well — which is the point of having multiples at all — while making it arithmetically impossible for anyone to accumulate without limit. That is a progressive ceiling achieved with one number and no means-testing, no tiers, and no discretion.

The ceiling needs no wage rate to enforce, so it applies identically to a contributor in Tier 1 Germany and Tier 3 Uganda. Only the payout differs — and only where a payout is possible at all. The equity is in the unit; the locality is in the settlement; the restraint is in the ceiling. Keep 9999 exactly as specified.

§ 10

Master of Thought — synthesis

The table was assembled to answer “what does an hour pay everywhere?” It answers a better question instead: “can pay be the unit at all?” — and the answer is no, permanently, for 42.1% of jurisdictions. That is not a disappointing result. It is the result that makes the design simple.

웃 is the minimum-wage hour — a unit of value, not a count of hours. It needs no table to mint, works in every jurisdiction including the thirteen that publish nothing, cannot inflate across five centuries, and cannot be bought — which is the same property that makes it un-hoardable and non-security. The equity argument and the coverage argument turn out to be the same argument, and the wage table becomes what it should always have been: a convenience at the edge, consulted when someone wants local currency, and honest about which of three tiers produced the number.
