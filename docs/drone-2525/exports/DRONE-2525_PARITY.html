0 · The three sentences that govern everything else

THE VECTOR LAW. Black ground, edges only. No fill, no gradient, no shading, no texture. A surface is never painted — it is implied by its edges. One saturated stroke per object from the thirteen. Depth is read from perspective and overlap, never from a brightness ramp. Every curve is a declared n-gon, so its cost is stated rather than implicit. The HUD is drawn in the same stroke language at the same weight — not chrome sitting above a 3D view.

THE SENSOR IS PAID FIRST. The CNN reserve is subtracted from the frame budget before the renderer gets any of it. The picture is never made prettier by starving the thing that sees. When the budget will not cover the reference stream, the unit degrades in a fixed order — sensor module, then resolution, then frame rate — and names the rung it fell to. A silent drop is the defect the ladder exists to prevent.

SILENCE IS NEVER CONSENT. An AI-initiated shot never fires without a named human approval event, at any authority level. What scales up the ladder is the breadth of one decision and who makes it — never whether a decision happened.

1.1 · The MoT ladder — 25 rungs

Band (the first digit) is the edge-compute class: it adds a sensor module, and each module costs CNN time. Two seats, two eyes. The pilot looks out of the canopy; the targeteer looks down a gimbal slung under the belly. The offsets are fractions of the airframe's own measured length, so an aircraft of another size gets its parallax for free and no constant has to be remembered.
