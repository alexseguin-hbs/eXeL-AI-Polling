## 1. The name form — follow his latest words
`<label>_<NNNN>.jpg` with an underscore: `bird_0001.jpg`, its pair `bird_0001.xml`. This replaces the dot form (`head.0001`) in the
17.09 note. At least 4 digits; past 9999 it grows (`bird_10000`). One form everywhere: page, Python program, XML `<filename>`.

## 2. One person's pictures stay in one unbroken block
His example: one member's 1,111 pictures are `bird_0001`–`bird_1111`; the next member's 1,111 are `bird_1112`–`bird_2222`. Each
member's pictures are one run of numbers, in the order they were taken, and the runs follow one another with no overlap and no gaps.

**How to guarantee it — final numbers are given once, at Close:**
1. **While collecting, names are temporary.** Each device saves pictures under a temporary name that can never clash, for example the
   member's short id plus their own count (`bird~ax~0001`), with the capture time in the XML. Members can work offline.
2. **The owner presses Close upload.** The project then numbers everything in one pass:
   - members in the order they joined (or first uploaded — the owner picks once per project);
   - within a member, pictures in the order they were taken;
   - member 1 gets `bird_0001`…`bird_N1`, member 2 continues at `bird_(N1+1)`, and so on.
3. **Every renaming is recorded** in that picture's XML (temporary name → final name, who, when), so nothing about its origin is lost.
4. **After Close the numbers never change.** A picture deleted later leaves a gap; its number is not reused.
5. **A second round of collecting** (re-open, or a new round) continues after the highest number: round 2 starts at `bird_2223`, again
   in member blocks.

Why not reserve blocks up front (e.g. "you get 1,111 numbers")? Nobody knows ahead how many pictures they will take; reserved blocks
leave gaps or run out. Numbering at Close gives exactly his example, unbroken, every time.

## 3. The project phases — one at a time, shown at the top of the project
| Phase | Who | What happens | What is locked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Collect** | all contributors | upload pictures, upload video (fewest different frames), live capture — temporary names | nothing |
| **2 Close upload** | owner | duplicates across members resolved; final numbers given in member blocks; the set is frozen | no new pictures |
| **3 Level 1 — annotate as a team** | contributors | the shared queue: each picture held for one person; boxes drawn; one XML per picture written beside it | pictures, names |
| **4 Level 2 — review as a team** | reviewers (never their own boxes) | accept · fix · reject; names merged; fine-tuning | Level 1 work is read-only except through review |
| **5 Export** | owner | only Level 2 boxes go to the training set and the next model folder | everything, as a released version |

1. **Level 2 starts after Level 1 is done** — the owner moves the phase when every picture has a Level 1 pass (or chooses to start
   reviewing early, picture by picture, if the team wants overlap; one switch, the owner's).
2. **The XMLs are written after Close**, so `<filename>` always holds the final name — no XML ever points at a temporary name.
3. **The phase is visible to everyone** on the project's step strip (the six original icons), with counts: *1,850 pictures · 1,200
   boxed · 300 reviewed*.
