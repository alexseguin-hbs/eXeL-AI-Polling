### The cadence, and why the fix is structural

Counted in **distinct chapters genuinely read since the last invitation** — not transitions (flipping
between two chapters would rack them up) and not completions (a skimmer would never be invited). The book is
12 chapters × 13 pages, stable across all 12 book languages, so today's "12 pages" already meant about one
chapter. A chapter joins `seen` at `pageIndex >= 3` — past the intro and two pages, a read and not a bounce.

| | threshold | cooldown | session cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| signed out | 1 chapter | 4 min | 4 |
| signed in | 3 chapters | 20 min | 2 |

**Reset ON SHOW, not on dismiss — that is the whole fix.** The trigger consumes its own quantity the moment
the modal opens, so no exit path (the ×, a Stripe cancel, a future "not now") needs any logic, and therefore
none can be forgotten. FIX THE CLASS, rather than a `dismissed` boolean that leaves the monotonic counter in
place for the next feature to re-arm.

### A correction to my own earlier reading, recorded before it could mislead

I had assumed `innovation-store.ts`'s `saveState()` was the reuse. **It is not usable here.** It takes no
owner argument and resolves one *internally* from a per-browser UUID with no auth branch, so a reader signing
in on a second device would get a different key and `loadState` would return null — the page would promise a
kept place and deliver nothing, which is exactly the "never claim more than happened" failure. The new store
exists only because the owner key must be derivable from the account, which that signature cannot express.

### The honest limit, stated now rather than discovered later

**This frontend never authenticates to Supabase.** The only `auth.getSession()` call in the app is in
SensorFusion and can never pass, so a new table's `authenticated` policy is inert and identity must travel as
an `owner_key` argument — a **bearer capability over a derived identifier** (an Auth0 `sub` is not secret
entropy). That is why the row holds three integers, two ids and a language code: no name, no email, no reader
text, and the derivation is never reused for anything holding a person's words.
