## 📋 Q1 — The divisor. Blocking every number in the document.

> **Context for review.** Vision • 2525 prices its entry token in *time*, not money, so the buy-in
> costs the same slice of life everywhere. The rule: **one Seed Token = one local statutory
> minimum-wage hour ÷ 7** = 8 minutes 34 seconds. Texas ($7.25/hr) → $1.04. Nigeria ($0.34/hr) →
> $0.05. A prior release divided by 7.25 instead, purely so Texas landed on exactly $1.00.
>
> **Question.** Is a globally uniform divisor (÷7, no country special-cased, Texas at $1.04) better
> than a divisor chosen to make one country's price a round number (÷7.25, Texas exactly $1.00)?
> What is the strongest argument for the round-number anchor that we may be missing? Does anchoring
> a global unit to one nation's currency create a bias that outweighs the memorability?

**Our position:** uniform ÷7. A round number at one point on earth makes that country the reference
frame and every other country a conversion of it. **Shipped at r71, flagged as unconfirmed.**

## 📋 Q2 — Most of the world has no single minimum wage. What do we do?

> **Context.** The token price divides a *local statutory minimum wage* by 7. We tested six
> jurisdictions. Only Brazil resolved cleanly. The other five failed in four distinct ways:
>
> | Failure | Where | What breaks |
> |---|---|---|
> | No anchor | **Dubai (UAE)**, **Singapore** | No statutory minimum wage exists. Singapore has sector-specific wage ladders only — so the rule would price a cleaner but not a clerk. |
> | Wrong unit | **Beijing** | Minimum wage is set municipally; there is no national figure. |
> | Not one number | **India** | Minimums vary by state, sector *and* skill category — hundreds of lawful answers. |
> | Two floors | **Moscow** | A federal floor and a regional supplement, both statutory, that disagree. |
>
> **Question.** For a voluntary global system that must price participation identically everywhere
> without becoming a political actor: how should it price a jurisdiction that has no single lawful
> wage floor? Candidate answers we see — (a) it cannot be priced until a floor exists; (b) use the
> lowest lawful rate in that jurisdiction; (c) use a published third-party index; (d) let the local
> group declare a floor on the record with their name attached. What are we not seeing? Which
> option is least capturable by a government or an employer?

**Our position:** (a) for no-anchor cases — it is honest and it is already the argument in §15,
where a state that sets a floor makes its own people countable. (b) for the other three, because
"lowest lawful" is checkable and never overstates. **Not implemented. Awaiting the operator.**

## 📋 Q3 — Is this instrument a security? Attack the design.

> **Context.** Contributors may give a small sum (≈$1) and receive a **Seed Token**. We need it to
> fall outside the definition of a security. The design, as written:
>
> - **Non-transferable** — cannot be sold, traded, gifted, assigned, pledged as collateral, or
>   transferred on death. No secondary market exists or ever will. No market → no price → nothing
>   to expect.
> - **Refund, never return** — if the framework shuts down, unspent reserves are returned **capped
>   at what each contributor put in, never a cent more**.
> - **No gain possible** — a per-person ceiling forbids accumulation and binds the founders first,
>   so gain is structurally unavailable rather than merely unpromised.
> - **Grants only** the right to buy an annual membership, which itself pays nothing and returns
>   nothing — it delivers news and updates.
> - **Denominated in time**, settled in local currency.
> - What a contributor receives is *the record that they seeded this*. Provenance, not property.
>
> **Question — argue against us.** Under the Howey test (or your jurisdiction's equivalent), where
> is this most vulnerable? We concede money changes hands and that a common enterprise is arguable;
> we believe "expectation of profit" is where the whole defence lives. **What would a regulator
> reach for that we have not closed?** Are there jurisdictions where non-transferability plus a
> capped refund is still insufficient? Does using crypto rails for settlement create exposure even
> with no tradeable instrument?

**Our position:** design intent, not a legal opinion. It needs counsel per jurisdiction and the
document says so. **Shipped at r70. §7 still describes the reserve claim without the cap — the two
disagree and the operator must confirm the cap.**

## 📋 Q4 — Execution without government, investor, or corporate interference

> **Context.** The claim: this can be started and run without permission from any state, investor
> or corporation. Seven structural reasons — (1) an hour of voluntary effort is not a licensed
> activity anywhere; (2) money may fund but never govern, so capture by capital is structurally
> impossible; (3) the ceiling means there is nothing worth capturing; (4) multi-jurisdiction by
> construction, so no single state can switch off a record not resident in it; (5) the off switch
> and reserve survive a hostile takeover of the organisation; (6) a pod starts at one person,
> below the threshold at which any of the three notices; (7) the document is a self-contained file
> that works offline, so distribution cannot be blocked.
>
> **Question.** Which of those seven is weakest? What stops this that we have not listed? We have
> named: no contributors · a fork of the reserve · a jurisdiction criminalising the payout rail ·
> a regulator reaching anyway · the training ladder never being built. **What is the sixth thing?**

**Our position:** the honest counterweight is in the document, not hidden. **Threading through the
sections is not done.**

## 📋 Q5 — The minimum-wage flywheel: does this argument hold?

> **Context.** Because the price is a slice of a *local statutory* minimum-wage hour, a country
> without one has no anchor — its people cannot be priced on the same terms, so they cannot join
> on the same terms. Not excluded by anybody; simply never written down. Our claim: a state that
> establishes a floor makes its own people countable, and gains a live, auditable measure of what
> an hour is worth inside its borders, produced by nobody with a reason to flatter it.
>
> **Question.** Is this a genuine incentive or wishful thinking? Could it be read as economic
> coercion of poor states? Does it invert the usual "how do you avoid government interference"
> problem — needing nothing from a government while offering it something — or does it create a
> dependency we have not seen? How would a hostile minister characterise it?

**Our position:** it is the strongest available answer to the government question because it is not
adversarial. **Shipped at r70.**
