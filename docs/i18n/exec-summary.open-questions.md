# Executive Summary — unresolved wording questions

Deliverable 3 of the canonicalization commission. Each item below was **recommended but not taken**,
because taking it would have changed meaning rather than grammar, and the operator's instruction was
*"Do not rewrite the philosophy. Do not add new doctrine. Perform a terminology-and-grammar
normalization pass only."* Each needs a one-word ruling.

---

## 1 — `sacred` or `fundamental`? (k49, Section 10)

**Stands today:**
> The living framework defines Master of Thought as a responsibility rather than a rank; that
> distinction should remain **sacred**.

**Recommended instead:** …should remain **fundamental**.

**The argument for changing it:** the executive edition is the public door. *Sacred* pulls the
paragraph into a spiritual register that the surrounding prose does not otherwise use, and a first
reader who is not already inside the framework may read it as a claim about religion rather than about
governance.

**The argument for keeping it:** the Divinity Guide is cited two sections earlier, Spiritual
Intelligence is one of the four intelligences named in Section 03, and the closing principles use that
register deliberately. *Sacred* may be the honest word.

**Why it was not taken:** it sits in a closing principle, which the ruling protects.

**Third option:** keep *sacred* in English and let each translation use its language's neutral
equivalent — the word carries noticeably heavier religious weight in Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi and Nepali
than it does in English.

## 2 — The seal's claim about cuneiform (k70)

**Stands today:**
> The Master of Thought seal — an eagle within a ring of cuneiform, **the oldest writing we still have**

**Recommended instead:** …*linking a modern responsibility to one of Humanity's oldest surviving
traditions of recorded thought.*

**The argument for changing it:** the current line is historically overbroad. Cuneiform is among the
earliest known writing systems, but whether it is *the* oldest surviving writing depends on how one
counts Egyptian hieroglyphs and the proto-writing that precedes both. A five-century document that asks
to be judged on evidence should not open a flank on a checkable fact.

**Why it was not taken:** the ruling says preserve the closing seal, and this is a factual/content edit
rather than a grammatical one.

## 3 — Section 02's formal definition of all three terms (k23)

**Recommended addition:**
> Vision • 2525 calls that path Thought Mastery. A person who practises it is a Thought Master. Master
> of Thought is the enduring responsibility behind both: to remain aware of thought before it becomes
> consequence.

**The argument for it:** it establishes the vocabulary once and early, so the fourteen later uses read
naturally instead of asking the reader to infer the hierarchy.

**Why it was not taken:** it adds a sentence. The ruling forbids new prose, and the operator's answer
was *"we are talking executive summary only!"* The hierarchy is currently carried by consistent usage,
which works but teaches more slowly.

**Cost if taken:** roughly 40 words. Safe — the Executive Summary sits in its own `EXEC_ORDER`, which
the sacred-totals check does not read, so 3,333 · 9,999 · 77,777 cannot move.

## 4 — Section 11's closing sentence (k52)

**Stands today:**
> The future advances when children do not merely know more than we knew, but can understand how we
> learned — and therefore surpass us.

**Recommended instead:**
> A future generation advances when it does not merely know more than we knew, but understands how we
> learned — and therefore knows how to surpass us.

**Why it was not taken:** *children* → *a future generation* is a change of referent, not of grammar.
The narrower word may be the deliberate one — it is warmer, and Section 11 is about education.

## 5 — The bullet counting law, and what it actually costs

Not a wording question, but it belongs on the same ruling sheet.

The operator's law is **"• is not a word, never count • as a word"** — the bullet in **Vision • 2525**
stands for sun, moon and earth across past, present and future, and is a mark rather than a token. The
expectation attached to it was that adopting the wordmark everywhere would need **no totals
adjustment**.

**Measured, and that expectation does not hold.** Excluding `•` and `·` from the word counters drops
the three reading totals to:

| View | Today | With bullets excluded | Shortfall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Brief | 3,333 | 3,319 | **14** |
| N.O.S.E. | 9,999 | 9,979 | **20** |
| White paper | 77,777 | 77,563 | **214** |

Those are bullets **already sitting inside counted prose** and counted as words when the text was
originally fitted. So the law is not free in either direction:

- **Adopt it** → 248 words of real prose must be authored to re-fit the three totals. After that,
  converting the 95 bare `Vision 2525` occurrences to `Vision • 2525` costs nothing, forever.
- **Leave it** → the totals hold untouched, but every bullet added to the wordmark adds a counted word,
  so the branding conversion itself would need an offsetting cut of the same size.

The counters are currently left as they were (standalone dashes excluded, bullets counted), the gate
passes at 3,333 · 9,999 · 77,777, and `_measure.mjs` now agrees with it exactly. **Nothing was silently
re-fitted.** This one is the operator's call.
