Six of the nine projects failed his bar — the six whose ledger carries no commit sha on its newest row, plus

## The cause, and why it was structural rather than clerical

A ledger row is written **in** the commit that carries it, so at the moment of writing nobody can know that
commit's sha. The deck solves this with its two-commit chain and a records commit that fills `shipped`
afterwards. The nine React ledgers have no such step, so the newest row stays blank for ever — and the newest
row is the one he opens first.

## Green — the generator resolves it from the ledger's own history

`gen-rcore-code.mjs` now answers an empty, `PENDING` or non-ancestor commit with the commit that **introduced
that row into its ledger** (`git log -1 -S'"rev": N' -- <ledger>`), and records `commitSource: "ledger-row"` on
the row so the record never silently claims a sha the ledger did not carry. It is self-healing: a row appended
today gets its code on the next generate.
