# S1 — Executive summary
## Purpose
Help cities, critical infrastructure operators, healthcare systems, and campuses move from fragmented crisis response to verified, transparent, resource-aware coordination.
## One-sentence product
De-Risking Strategies is the human-directed coordination layer that sits beside CrisisCommand and specialist operational systems so leaders can verify who is in command, see real needs, allocate scarce resources, publish the current plan, and replay what happened.
## Core value proposition
For organizations responsible for people, infrastructure, and essential resources during high-consequence disruptions, De-Risking Strategies connects crisis command, validated leadership, community and field input, resources, and operational learning in one human-directed system — so leaders can see what is happening, adapt the plan, communicate changes, allocate scarce resources, and preserve what happened for the next event.
## What this is not
This is not a grid-management system, a hospital incident-command suite, a mass-notification product, or a replacement for CrisisCommand.
GridOS operates the grid.Veoci runs the hospital emergency program.Sentinel notifies and convenes the campus.CrisisCommand structures strategy, stakeholders, messages, and SITREPs.
DRS coordinates the human and resource crisis around those systems.

# S5 — Customer problem
## Problem statement
During disruption, authority, needs, resources, and plan changes live in different systems and in human memory. Leaders cannot reliably answer who is allowed to act, what is actually needed, what is actually available, where it went, who changed the plan, and what the workforce or public should do now.
Today’s response environment is distributed among specialist systems, emergency procedures, phone calls, spreadsheets, messaging platforms, government channels, and human memory. That fragmentation creates uncertainty about authority, poor visibility of real needs, duplicated or misallocated resources, citizen or employee panic, hoarding or opportunistic use of scarce resources, difficulty distinguishing legitimate responders, weak traceability when plans change, and lost institutional learning.
Project Resilience (6 December 2023) framed the same underlying problem as real-time information gaps, inefficient resource coordination, and limited public communications during emergency events. That lineage stands. The 2023 CONOPS opened with “real-time alerts.” That job is now owned by NWS, OMS, Sentinel, and Everbridge. DRS keeps roster, needs, resources, the public plan card, and replay.
## Customer outcomes
A single current plan with named authority.
Needs and resources as objects, not chat threads.
Controlled transparency — enough truth to reduce panic, not so much that it creates it.
An after-event record that can be replayed rather than reconstructed from inboxes.
Enhanced convenience in finding help, shelter, and supplies.
Greater access to assistance that is actually available.
## Customer whys
Liability and legitimacy: uncredentialed helpers and conflicting spokespeople.
Scarcity: hoarding, double-assigning, and invisible gaps.
Trust: generic alerts feel irrelevant; silence feels like abandonment.
Learning: the next storm should not restart from zero.
## Problems with the status quo, mapped
GridOS restores electrons. It does not own community medical-device or shelter demand. Storm Manager does coordinate restoration crews. DRS does not claim that job.
Veoci runs the hospital’s incident, HICS documentation, and accreditation AAR. Regional load and the public narrative still fragment.
Sentinel tells the campus something happened. It does not issue blankets or open the right building.
CrisisCommand produces the strategy and the messages. It does not move pallets.

# S7 — Workflow and personas
## Command and accountability
Incident commander. City official. Utility executive. Plant manager. Hospital executive. Campus leader.
They already live in ICS / HICS boards, CrisisCommand, and the EOC huddle. They want one current plan, named deputies, and no surprise spokespeople.
## Operational responders
Emergency management. Facilities. Medical personnel. Maintenance. Security. Logistics. Qualified volunteers.
They already live in OMS, CMMS, badge systems, radio, Veoci rooms, and Sentinel chat. They want clean assignments, inventory truth, and fewer collisions.
## Affected population
Residents. Employees. Patients and families. Students and parents.
They already live in blast alerts, PIO channels, and local media. They want honest status, a way to request help, and no rumor mill. They receive a role-limited view. They do not receive operational authority.
## Integrator
IT, identity, dispatch. They already live in the IdP and SIEM. They want a source of truth for identity, uptime, audit, and no shadow AI.
## System principle
Public participants do not receive the same authority as operational leaders.
The platform maintains a validated roster and rank structure.
AI recommends and organizes. Authorized humans retain command.
## User stories
MVP 1.0 CORE — As incident commander, I want a verified roster with rank, so unofficial helpers cannot issue orders.
MVP 1.1 — As logistics lead, I want needs and resources as objects, so I can assign without a spreadsheet.
MVP 1.2 — As PIO or campus communications, I want the current plan card, so I do not publish a superseded message.
MVP 1.3 — As a member of the affected population, I want a role-limited view of what to do now, so I am not inside the operations picture.
MVP 2.0 — As EM director, I want eXeL concern polling, so emergent needs appear before they become 911 load.
MVP 2.1 — As readiness owner, I want replay of plan versions against actual fulfillment, so the debrief is not reconstructed from inboxes.
MVP 3.0 — As system operator, I want Conservative / Baseline / Aggressive resource simulations before the next season, so we pre-commit reserves with named triggers.

# S11 — R-CORE relationship
R-CORE is not the crisis application.
It is the recursive operational architecture beneath it.
Vision 2525 defines R-CORE as a coordination architecture connecting communications, coordination, intelligence, replay, simulation, qualification, and continual improvement while preserving human judgment and transparent authority.
One-line definition for readers who have not read Vision 2525: R-CORE is the recursive operational architecture that connects live coordination to a durable operational truth, then forces replay, simulation, qualification, and improvement without removing human command.
## Loop
LIVE EVENT — signals, people, resources→ COORDINATE — command, needs, assignments→ SYNC / OPERATIONAL TRUTH — who knew what, who authorized what, what changed, what moved, what happened→ REPLAY — reconstruct the event→ SIMULATE — Conservative, Baseline, Aggressive→ QUALIFY — measure readiness→ NEXT EVENT — better prepared
## Core statement
Every crisis becomes evidence. Every replay improves readiness. Every cycle strengthens resilience.
## What R-CORE is not
Not a fourth product brand to sell on day one.Not an autonomous commander.Not a data-lake slogan.
Named artifacts: authority, version, assignment, fulfillment, delta, readiness score.

# S15 — Three resource plans
Signature feature.
|  | Conservative | Baseline | Aggressive |
| Assumption | Demand at the low end of history; high uncertainty | Expected demand from the last comparable event | High-impact tail; pre-position surge |
| Posture | Minimum essential deployment | Balanced response | Maximum surge capacity |

The labels are not the product. Provenance is.
