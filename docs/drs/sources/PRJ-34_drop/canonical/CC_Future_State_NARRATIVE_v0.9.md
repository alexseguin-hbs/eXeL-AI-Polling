## 1. Future-state vision

CrisisCommand evolves from a structured crisis decision workflow into a governed decision-to-action-and-learning operating system for leadership.

It does not replace emergency notification, technical incident response, recall systems, business continuity platforms, legal systems, or other specialist operational tools. Those systems remain systems of record for the work they already do well.

CrisisCommand sits above them as the leadership layer that answers:

- What do we know?
- What does it mean?
- What matters most now?
- Who has authority to decide?
- What decision was made?
- Why was it made?
- Who owns the next action?
- Has that action been acknowledged or completed?
- What should each stakeholder hear?
- What changed as the crisis evolved?
- What did the organization learn?

**Operating loop**

Operational truth → executive judgment → approved decision → named action → evidence → stakeholder response → replay → improvement

**Outcome**

Not merely faster communication.  
Faster time to *coherent, approved leadership action*, with a traceable record of what was known, what was decided, why it changed, who owned the action, and what happened next.

## 3. Product boundary

Specialist systems keep:

Emergency alerts and public-safety comms · technical incident response · service restoration · recall / quality records · operational task execution · clinical records · physical resource management · legal matter systems

CrisisCommand owns:

Strategic picture · objectives · cross-domain exposure · stakeholder priorities · leadership tradeoffs · decision rights · approvals · rationale · action ownership · approved communications posture · escalation watchpoints · current plan state · replay and learning

Operational truth comes in. Human judgment happens here. Approved action goes out. Evidence comes back. Learning compounds.

AI recommends and organizes. Humans approve and own consequences.

This is the original HI design. The segment is the *need*. The industry edition is only the beachhead. Each need has its own current-stack alternative. Do not collapse them into one “crisis software” competitor.

RETAIL, HEALTH, and FIN are live editions. They are not in the first validation wave. Utility is archived from the DRS-attach study. Sentinel is discovery-only on EDU unless a named buyer has it in the stack.

“EDU has the lowest CTS” is not a fact.  
Sequence is not TAM.

### 4.1 EDU — positioning

Emergency systems help the institution communicate and respond to the event.  
CrisisCommand helps leadership decide what the institution will do, why, who owns it, and how that posture remains coherent as the crisis changes.

Buyer hypothesis: President / Chancellor / COO / CCO.  
Operating: comms, GC, chief of staff, EM leadership.  
Primary clock: event → president / cabinet approved posture.  
Do not monetize student or public safety itself.  
Do not claim better alerting than Rave / Everbridge.

### 4.2 FOOD — positioning

Recall and CEM systems manage the incident.  
CrisisCommand manages the leadership judgment around what the incident means for the enterprise and its stakeholders.

Buyer hypothesis: COO / CCO / GC / Quality.  
Primary clock: material product fact → enterprise severity / recall posture approved.

### 4.3 TECH — positioning

PagerDuty runs the service incident.  
CrisisCommand runs the institution’s leadership response to what that incident means.

Buyer hypothesis: COO / CCO / CISO / CIO / GC.  
Primary clock: technical severity established → executive / customer / regulator posture approved.  
Do not claim CrisisCommand shortens technical outage duration unless a customer proves causality.

## 5. Why these three NBAs must stay distinct

Early drafts confused the stack. That produced Utility↔GridOS, Healthcare↔Veoci, Campus↔Sentinel — the Resilience attach map — and then a second confusion that treated Everbridge as the only alternative in every segment.

Hold the fences:

| If someone says… | Answer |
|---|---|
| “Our NBA is Everbridge” | For EDU and FOOD, Everbridge is *part* of the stack. The rest of the job is cabinet, counsel, QMS, or recall. |
| “Our NBA is PagerDuty” | Only for the TECH need, and only for the technical half. The executive half is still manual. |
| “Our NBA is Sentinel” | Only if that campus actually runs Sentinel. Default EDU stack is Rave / Everbridge + cabinet + counsel. |
| “We should lead with GridOS / Veoci” | That was the retired DRS physical-coordination thesis. Not this future state. |
| “Coordination / locked plans / AI next steps are unique” | Everbridge 360 AI / Bridge already claims those. Not unique. |
| “Incident comms and status are unique” | PagerDuty already owns those. Not unique. |

CrisisCommand territory that can still be tested: executive judgment · sector precedent inside the live workflow · organization-specific context · leadership alignment · stakeholder strategy · *explicit* decision rights and approval state · outcome-linked replay connected to specialist systems.

Generic claims that are not unique: better decisions · AI advisor · continuous improvement · crisis management · executive dashboards · alerts · tasks · locked plans by themselves.

## 6. How value will be measured

Time-to-coherence is the internal metric. Dollar-per-minute is a formula for interviews and S14 notes, not an S1 savings claim.

**Clocks**

- incident recognized → leadership activated  
- leadership activated → objectives approved  
- objectives approved → approved posture  
- approved posture → named action owner  
- approval → stakeholder release  
- material fact change → revised approved posture  

**Verified $/min** (customer inputs only)  
direct time-dependent cost ÷ elapsed minutes  
Labor, advisor burn, contact-center surge, incremental opex, attributable lost flow, SLA exposure.

**Time Value** = verified $/min × leadership-delay minutes removed  

**Risk-Adjusted Value** = (P before − P after) × consequence  
Range shown. Never a guaranteed saving.

**Modeled Value** = Time Value + discrete avoided cost + risk-adjusted outcome  
**Contribution** = validated WTP − CrisisCommand-specific CTS  

No price is supported until the buyer validates baseline time, CC-assisted time, delta, $/min or consequence, attribution, and willingness to pay.

Old DRS figures ($22k / $48k / $60k, $67M SAM, NPV/IRR) stay in archive. Do not print them here.
