## The twelve (≈111 words each)

**Aset — consistency.** The phantoms are gone: only my lane's targets stand on the picture, and the NO_PHANTOM_TARGETS row proves zero
grey silhouettes while the other lanes' targets still exist. But the sign is not consistent with the photo he sent. r.158 draws a
screen-space box that grows to fit "◂ 20 · 21 ▸", so a 300 m sign is as big as a 100 m sign and both dwarf the 50 m targets. Real lane
boards shrink with distance like everything else on the range. The dotted lane lines are consistent: faint, grey, post to post. Fix:
draw the board at its real projected size, white with black numbers, and let the numbers shrink with it.

**Asar — outcome.** The outcome he asked for in Addendum 6 was "lane markers on the actual sign"; r.158 delivered numbers on a board,
which is half the outcome. His follow-up screenshot shows the other half missing: the boards are oversized, outline-only, and two of
them sit on top of the 100 m and 150 m targets. An outcome that hides a target is a regression on a range, because the shooter's job is
to see the target first. The record side is fine: Train Up still counts hits and misses, the light is green, and nothing new reaches the
strip. Next: smaller signs that never touch a target, and white boards so they read as signs.

**Athena — flow.** At 1× the first thing the eye meets is three bright boxes with text, not the targets. That breaks the flow r.157 built
(TARGET is lit, the strip says TARGET FIRST): the player reads signs before finding the 50 m pair. The lane lines help orientation
without demanding attention, which is the right weight. The signs should sit at that same weight — small, white, at the edge of the lane,
readable when you zoom in, ignorable when you are shooting. On a PC the flow also stops at the mouse: a left click on the picture marks
instead of firing, and right click opens the browser menu. He named the PC controls; they belong in the next revision.

**Christo — first-time phone player.** I would not know which box is a target and which is a sign at 1×: both are outlined rectangles in
the same cream colour. White boards with black numbers separate them instantly, as on the real range. The phantoms were the worst
confusion and they are gone, which helps a first visit a lot. I still need to know the PC keys: CONTROLS lists them, but Space marks and
fires depending on state, which surprised me. One rule would teach itself: Space or right click marks, a second press approves, F or
left click fires. Keep the two steps; just make the keys say the same thing every time.

**Enki — edge cases.** Edge cases on the signs: when a sign's words would overlap another sign, r.158 drops the words but the enlarged box
still stands, so a 300 m box can sit over the 150 m target with no words at all. At 30× zoom the boxes balloon because the minimum text
size forces them; at the lane-1 outer edge the "1 ▸" board is narrower than its own words. A real-size board avoids every one of these:
it is small far away, legible up close, and if it would cover a target it can simply not be drawn. Also test the mouse on a touch
laptop: pointerType tells mouse from finger, so taps keep their r.131 rules.

**Enlil — built as specified.** Specified: "lane markers need to be on the actual sign <-- 20 21 -->"; built: the words on a board —
yes. Specified next: "smaller like uploaded images (realistic as photo uploaded)", "DO not cover targets", "White/Black" — not built
in r.158 because they arrived after it shipped. The gates were real: NO_PHANTOM_TARGETS counts drawn grey silhouettes, LANE_NUMBERS_ON_THE_SIGN
checked the words sit inside their board, LANE_LINES_SIGN_TO_SIGN checks four lines on my two edges. What the gates did not check is the
operator's actual complaint — a sign over a target. Add a row that fails when any drawn board touches a standing target's rectangle.

**Krishna — integration.** The sign touches four systems: the marker geometry (laneMarkers), the world draw (posts), the HUD painter
(board + words) and the overprint rules (taken rectangles). r.158 kept them in one function, which is good. The missing link is the
target layer: boardLabels avoids captions, the bullseye and the DOM strip, but not the silhouettes, which live in plateRect. One call —
"does this board touch a standing target of my lane?" — integrates them. The PC controls integrate the same way: Space and the right
click should share one door (target, else approve on amber), and left click should reuse fireN so its refusals stay identical to FIRE.

**Odin — future.** Signs and lines will matter more when rooms run 3v3 and 9v9 on adjacent lanes: each shooter must find their own lane
from numbers alone. Real-size boards scale to that future; screen-sized boxes would multiply clutter with every lane in view. The PC
controls matter for the same future — instructors and spectators will run the range from laptops, and mouse play is the natural
operator console. Keep the doctrine fixed while inputs multiply: every input reaches TARGET → APPROVE → FIRE through the same functions,
so a new controller can never add a third path. The dotted lines are a good base for the future lane-assignment overlay.

**Pangu — real range.** A real known-distance or pop-up range has small white lane boards with black numbers on posts, readable through
optics, not at a glance from the firing line. The photo he sent shows exactly that ("5 6" on a white board over a green E-type). r.158's
outlined boxes read like UI, not like range furniture. White fill and black digits fix the look; real size fixes the scale; never
covering a target follows how a range is laid out (boards sit beside the lanes, never in front of the targets). The faint lane lines are
a good stand-in for the lane flags and markers a real range uses on the ground.

**Sofia — plain words.** The sign text "◂ 20 · 21 ▸" is plain and matches his own words. The status line stays plain ("TRAIN UP · HIT 0 ·
MISS 0", "LANE 21 · TRAIN UP · EVERY TARGET UP · A HIT COMES BACK UP"). The only new wording risk is on the PC: the right click and
Space need one sentence when the state does not allow the action, e.g. "RED · LEFT CLICK OR F TO FIRE" when the mark is already red. No
raw ids appeared in my walk. When a sign is too small to read, show the white board alone rather than squeezing illegible text — a
blank board is honest; a smudge of tiny digits is not.

**Thoth — numbers.** Measured: 343 segments drawn at 1× (r.157: 585, with 348 dropped) — the phantoms cost 242 lines, so removing them also
fixed the budget. A real lane board is about 0.6 × 0.35 m. At 1× on a 12-inch phone (1827 px focal) that is 11 × 6 px at 100 m, 5 × 3 px at
200 m; at 3×, 33 × 19 px at 100 m — numbers legible only when zoomed, which is realistic. r.158's box was at least 92 × 16 px whatever the
range: up to 30 times too large at 300 m. Boot QA 195/196 both orientations; full test:ci and build green.

**Thor — risk.** The risk on a range is anything that hides a target or misleads the shooter. r.158's oversized signs did both in the
operator's screenshot. Removing the phantoms removed a worse risk: grey shapes a player could aim at that were not on their lane. The
PC controls carry a doctrine risk: a left click that fires must not skip APPROVE — it must call fireN, which refuses anything not red
— and the right click must never fire. One shared door for Space and right click, with a QA row proving three presses go target →
approve → "fire with F", keeps the two-step rule intact on every input.

## MoT summary (overall C+, SSSES 74.3)

r.158 fixed the worst thing he reported — the grey phantoms — and proved it with a row that counts drawn silhouettes. It put the numbers
on a board and drew the lane lines. But it built the sign as UI rather than range furniture: outlined, screen-sized, and able to sit on
a target, which his screenshot shows. All twelve lenses converge on one fix and one addition. The fix: real-size white boards with black
numbers that shrink with distance, never drawn over a standing target (with a gate for exactly that). The addition: his PC controls —
arrows/WASD as today, Space or right click = TARGET then APPROVE, F or left click = FIRE, all through the existing doors so the two-step
doctrine cannot be bypassed. Both are in r.159.
