## 6 · The single platform — which ONE NBA should be the platform benchmark and why (Rave / Everbridge 360 AI–Bridge / PagerDuty / other); the strongest value proposition in ONE sentence; what "every aspect of the existing NBA inside CrisisCommand.ai" means at the 2027 / 2030 / 2525 horizons (three lines)

**Benchmark: Everbridge 360 AI / Bridge.** From the edge-case seat it is the only choice that survives. Rave is a hardware incumbent's public-safety product — sirens, campus devices, first-responder and government wireless-alert rails — and absorbing it means becoming a public-safety vendor in one segment; PagerDuty's buyer is the engineering leader, not the executive, and its job (on-call, remediation, hundreds of technical integrations) is one the leadership platform should consume, not own. Everbridge is the one NBA already present in two of the three stacks (the narrative lists it in EDU and FOOD; it also sells IT alerting), its buyer is already the resilience and communications executive, and the deck itself admits it already claims coordination, locked plans and AI next steps — so it is the incumbent whose whole job CrisisCommand must carry, with a governed head Everbridge does not have. The break the platform must respect: delivery rails (carrier text, voice, government alerts) are embedded commodities every critical-event vendor resells; the platform owns the workflow, the record, the send and the learning, never the towers.

**Value proposition:** CrisisCommand.ai is the one platform where a leadership team runs the whole critical event — facts, severity, decision rights, approval, named action, the message to every stakeholder in their language, evidence and replay — so no institution needs a second system to decide, act, tell and learn, and a human owns every consequence.

**2027** — every critical-event job in the benchmark runs inside CrisisCommand: intake, severity, decision rights, approval, action ownership, acknowledgement and the send itself over embedded rails; a customer with only CrisisCommand runs the whole event, and the benchmark's remaining features are a closing checklist, measured by one A/B with a no-tooling control.
**2030** — risk intelligence, continuity planning, multi-instance decision graph, simulation and readiness qualification live in the same record, in every stakeholder language, resident in the customer's region; Rave and PagerDuty are sensors; the platform earns its own autonomy capability by capability through the one-ninth gate.
**2525** — the governed memory of how institutions decided under pressure, replayable across generations and borders; coordination without domination; the platform is a commons any institution on earth can join, and humanity still decides.

## 7 · 111-word summary — exactly 111 words, addressed to the founder, investors and future customers; count the words and print `words: 111` on the line after it

Founder, investors, future customers: this deck is honest about what CrisisCommand already does and careful never to let a machine decide. Its flaw is that it argues for a coordinating layer beside three vendors when the future is one platform that runs the whole event. From the edge, three NBAs are three products; one benchmark, Everbridge 360 AI / Bridge, is the job to carry, with a governed head it lacks. The deck forgets the buyer with no tooling, the trustee who signs, the counsel who fears the record, the crisis without a minute zero, and every institution outside the United States. Fix those, and small success becomes humanity's operating memory.
